RepresentDC

We asked every candidate on the June ballot about statehood.

Here’s who answered — and what they said.

Congress has introduced 74+ bills targeting DC — blocking $1.1 billion of our budget, overturning local laws, and stripping away home rule. The people who win in June will decide how DC responds.

So we asked. Every declared candidate for DC elected office and DC Democratic State Committee received a 7-question statehood questionnaire. 207 candidates. Every party. Every race.

29 responded • 100% support statehood • 178 still silent

Elected Office Candidates

19 of 103 candidates have responded. Here’s what they’re already doing.

“It was my legislation the Council passed to change DC’s license plates to read ‘End Taxation Without Representation’ — to remind everyone that statehood and autonomy will only be won through action, strategy, and organizing.”

Charles Allen · Ward 6 Council Member (incumbent)

“I have briefed Leader Pelosi, Leader Jeffries, and the Congressional Black Caucus seven times. I look forward to ensuring these members of Congress are solidified as allies in our efforts to achieve statehood.”

Greg Jackson · candidate for At-Large Council

“Let’s be clear: if the Republicans thought THEY would get 2 new Senators from making DC a state, we’d have been a state for years. We have to call out this hypocrisy — and find avenues beyond shame (because they have so little).”

Kinney Zalesne · candidate for Delegate to Congress

“We are watching democratic norms erode in real time. We are seeing the will of voters undermined, local decision-making attacked, and power consolidated in ways that silence communities. And I’m angry.”

Rashida Brown · candidate for Ward 1 Council

“Statehood must be framed not as a local preference, but as unfinished civil rights work and a critical democratic reform.”

Markus Batchelor · candidate for Shadow Senator

DC Democratic State Committee

104 candidates for the party positions that shape how Democrats organize for statehood nationally. 10 have responded.

“I have been arrested twice — in 1993 and 2011 — participating in demonstrations for DC statehood. In 1977, as president of the DC Young Democrats, I introduced the resolution that led to the Young Democrats of America endorsing DC statehood at its national convention — the first national Democratic Party organization to do so.”

Philip Pannell · candidate for National Committeeman (Free DC Slate)

“More than 15,000 fans chant ‘Free DC’ in the 51st minute of every Washington Spirit home match, symbolizing our demand for DC to become the 51st state. DC statehood will not be gifted from above — it will be won by the people who live here.”

Slobodan Milic · candidate for Ward 4 Committeeman (Free DC Slate)

“I’ve worked to elect over 50 lawmakers across the country who support statehood. Four of them currently sit on the Congressional committee that hosts hearings on home rule.”

Elizabeth Mitchell · candidate for Ward 3 Committeewoman (Free DC Slate)

“As soon as Congress introduces a bill that solely affects our local laws or money, then every member of Congress becomes our member of Congress. We should make it as painful as possible for Congress to continue to interfere in our local governance.”

Michelle Chappell · candidate for Ward 1 Committeewoman (Free DC Slate)

Jim Bubar, who filed amicus briefs before the U.S. Supreme Court for DC voting rights and fought to include statehood in the national Democratic platform, is running for At-Large Committeeman on the Democrats United to Free DC slate.

Here’s what you can do right now.

178 candidates still haven’t gone on the record. This June’s primary is a rare chance to get a large group of current and future DC leaders to commit — publicly — to defending our right to govern ourselves. That only happens if voters demand it.

At every candidate forum this spring, ask: “What specifically will you do to defend home rule and advance statehood?” Make them answer. Every time.

Know a candidate who hasn’t responded? Send them the tracker. The questionnaire takes 10 minutes and is still open. No one gets to sit this one out.

Share the tracker with your neighbors, your ANC listserv, your group chats. The more voters who see it, the harder it is for candidates to stay silent.
