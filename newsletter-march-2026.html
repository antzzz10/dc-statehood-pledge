What They've Already Done

Several respondents cited concrete past actions — not just promises. Here are standouts.

"I have been arrested twice — in 1993 and 2011 — participating in demonstrations for DC statehood."

— Philip Pannell, candidate for National Committeeman (Free DC Slate)

"Elected as a delegate and serving on the national platform committee, I fought to include Statehood in the national Democratic party platform."

— James S. Bubar, candidate for At-Large Committeeman (Democrats United to Free DC)

"As Chair of the DC Democratic Party, I successfully led the passage of a resolution in which Democratic Party leaders from all 50 states and the territories pledged their support for advancing DC Statehood."

— Charles E. Wilson, candidate for At-Large Committeeman (Democrats United to Free DC)

"I am proud to have drafted and secured a national resolution within the NAACP to reaffirm support for DC Statehood as a fundamental Democratic Right to Self-Determination. This moved the conversation from a local political issue to a national civil rights mandate."

— Nina Taylor, candidate for At-Large Council Member (Special Election)

"I led a delegation of nearly 50 Ward 5 neighbors to Capitol Hill to directly lobby our congressional representatives on DC statehood and home rule."

— Zachary Parker, Ward 5 Council Member (incumbent)

"I have defended the District's laws and local control. Not every DC elected official has shown up when it mattered, but I have."

— Charles Allen, Ward 6 Council Member (incumbent)

Worth Noting

On the real threat to Home Rule

"I am gravely concerned that the current President would like to return DC to a Board of Governors naming himself as Chair, abolishing Home Rule and both the City Council and Mayor's office in favor of a Board he appoints and Chairs. I believe this to be a real present danger."

— Terry Lynch, candidate for Ward 1 Council Member

Note: Lynch is not describing a hypothetical — bills to abolish DC's Home Rule and replace the elected government with a federally appointed Board of Governors have already been introduced in both the House and Senate in 2025. His concern reflects active legislation, not speculation.

On fighting the Mayor's own decision

"After the Mayor decided to repeal DC's sanctuary values law, I led the fight to place the Sanctuary Values Amendment Act on a Council vote to reverse her decision."

— Brooke Pinto, candidate for Delegate to the House of Representatives (current DC Council member)

On DC's untapped base of out-of-state allies

"I have encountered many new residents in DC who still maintain their right to vote in their hometowns. Often, they ask me how they can get involved in DC politics... Many people truly want to know how they can advocate for DC in states where their vote and their voice hold more power."

— Dyana Forester, candidate for At-Large Council Member

DC Democratic Party Committee Candidates

Response rates for party committee candidates were much lower than for elected office candidates — just 14 out of 105 (13%). The committee races are often lower-profile and outreach is harder, but these positions shape how the state party prioritizes and advocates for statehood at the national level.
