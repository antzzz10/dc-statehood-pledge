What They've Already Done

Several respondents cited concrete past actions — not just promises. Here are the standouts across a wide range of offices.

"It was even my legislation the Council passed to change DC's license plates to read 'End Taxation Without Representation' — to remind everyone that statehood and autonomy will only be won through action, strategy, and organizing."

— Charles Allen, Ward 6 Council Member (incumbent)

"I led a delegation of nearly 50 Ward 5 neighbors to Capitol Hill to meet directly with Members of Congress and their staff to lobby against harmful congressional action."

— Zachary Parker, Ward 5 Council Member (incumbent)

"I drafted and secured a national resolution within the NAACP to reaffirm support for DC Statehood as a fundamental Democratic Right to Self-Determination. This moved the conversation from a local political issue to a national civil rights imperative."

— Nina Taylor, candidate for At-Large Council Member (Special Election)

"As Executive Director of Long Live GoGo and Co-Founder of Free DC Project, I've helped lead and produce Statehood-focused cultural activations — from GoGo-powered demonstrations and Art All Night Statehood stages to national moments like the DNC — centering DC residents and our lived experience while making Statehood visible, accessible, and undeniable."

— Kelsye Adams, candidate for National Committeewoman (Free DC Slate)

"Elected as a delegate and serving on the national platform committee, I fought to include Statehood in the national Democratic party platform. As an attorney and co-chair of the Bar's DC Affairs Community, I filed amicus briefs before the US Supreme Court for equal Congressional voting rights."

— James S. Bubar, candidate for At-Large Committeeman (Democrats United to Free DC)

"As Co-Chair of Free DC's Congress Working Group, I have tracked dozens of legislative attacks on DC and directed hundreds of volunteers to call, email, and visit Congressional offices and attend hearings — creating a visible, vocal, sustained pro-Statehood presence on the Hill. I have spoken to many DC residents who came to Congressional offices to lobby for the first time in their lives."

— Michelle Chappell, candidate for Ward 1 Committeewoman (Free DC Slate)

"In 1977, as president of the DC Young Democrats, I introduced the resolution that led to the Young Democrats of America endorsing DC statehood at their national convention in Miami — the first national Democratic Party organization to endorse DC statehood. I have been arrested twice — in 1993 and 2011 — participating in demonstrations for statehood."

— Philip Pannell, candidate for National Committeeman (Free DC Slate)

"As Chair of the DC Democratic Party, I led the passage of a resolution in which Democratic Party leaders from all 50 states and territories pledged their support for DC Statehood. At the 2024 Democratic National Convention, we hosted a DC Statehood educational booth to engage attendees from across the country."

— Charles E. Wilson, candidate for At-Large Committeeman (Democrats United to Free DC)

Specific Plans — Not Just "I Support Statehood"

Several respondents went beyond general support and described concrete strategies — some substantive, some unconventional.

Substantive Strategies

Use the Home Rule Act's own deadlines against Congress

"The Home Rule Act mandates a strict 30-day layover period for Congress to review DC laws. If Congress attempts to block a law after this window has closed, I would immediately authorize the AG to sue, arguing the disapproval is null and void. Congress has used riders for decades to block DC from spending its own local funds — on marijuana regulation since 2014, on abortion services for decades, on needle exchanges for over 10 years. I would litigate to prevent that."

— David Gatling, candidate for Mayor

Don't wait for the perfect — pursue a voting House seat by statute now

"Congress has considered legislation that would grant DC a voting representative in the House by statute — treating DC 'as though it were a state' for purposes of House representation. Previous versions passed the House in 2007 and 2009. As Mayor, I will actively lobby for renewed Congressional action on this approach. We should not let the perfect be the enemy of progress."

— Gary Goodweather, candidate for Mayor

On the bills already introduced to abolish Home Rule

"I am gravely concerned that the current President would like to return DC to a Board of Governors naming himself as Chair, abolishing Home Rule and both the City Council and Mayor's office in favor of a Board he appoints and Chairs. I believe this to be a real present danger."

— Terry Lynch, candidate for Ward 1 Council Member

Note: Bills to do exactly this — abolish DC's elected government and replace it with a federally appointed Board of Governors — have already been introduced in both the House and Senate in 2025. Lynch is describing active legislation, not a hypothetical.

A standing Home Rule defense operation

"I will establish a standing Home Rule Defense Task Force composed of the Office of the Attorney General, my administration, and the Office of the Chief Financial Officer. This task force will proactively monitor congressional actions, coordinate legal strategies to challenge unlawful interference, and prepare fiscal contingency plans to protect the District's budget from federal disruption. Defending Home Rule cannot be reactive. It must be organized, strategic, and relentless."

— Kenyan R. McDuffie, candidate for Mayor

Neutralize the Senate-balance objection

"One idea that has been discussed nationally is pairing DC statehood with Puerto Rico statehood and admitting both at the same time. Historically, the United States has admitted states in pairs to address political balance concerns. Approaches like this may help break the current gridlock."

— Rini Sampath, candidate for Mayor

Make the cost of interference visible and immediate

"I will activate a 24-hour Home Rule Rapid Response: a public briefing, coordinated press with coalition partners, and a public 'harm receipt' explaining what the interference will cost DC residents in real terms — services, public safety, schools, and local economic stability."

— Sandi Stevens, candidate for Delegate to the House of Representatives

Name the hypocrisy — then find the bargain

"Let's be clear: if the Republicans thought THEY would get 2 new Senators from making DC a state, we'd have been a state for years. We have to call out this hypocrisy — and at the same time, find avenues beyond shame... a grand bargain that gives us statehood and Republicans something they want."

— Kinney Zalesne, candidate for Delegate to the House of Representatives

Creative Approaches

Disorient, don't just demonstrate

"Through theater, dance, playfulness, costumes, and strangeness, we can disorient the normal dynamics that let people ignore advocates."

— Sam Bonar, candidate for At-Large Committeeman (Free DC Slate)

Eliminate federal income tax until statehood

"I will introduce legislation to eliminate federal income tax requirements on DC residents until DC becomes a state, to achieve parity with other non-state portions of the US."

— Greg Jaczko, candidate for Delegate to the House of Representatives

Take the message to national TV

"I will pursue opportunities to elevate the cause at major national events, such as the Rose Bowl Parade and the Macy's Thanksgiving Parade. Millions of Americans tune into these moments. We must use them to tell DC's story."

— Brian Ready, candidate for US Shadow Representative

DC Democratic Party Committee Candidates

Response rates for party committee candidates were much lower than for elected office candidates — just 15 out of 105 (14%). The committee races are often lower-profile and outreach is harder, but these positions shape how the state party prioritizes and advocates for statehood at the national level.
